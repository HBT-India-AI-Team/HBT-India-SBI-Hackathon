# Digital Personal Data Protection Rules 2025

## Regulation: DPDP/2025/Rules

**Issued by:** Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY)  
**Date:** January 2025  
**Under:** Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023  
**Subject:** Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025

---

## Chapter 1: Preliminary

### Clause 1.1 – Short Title and Commencement
These rules may be called the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025. They come into force on the date of their publication in the Official Gazette.

### Clause 1.2 – Definitions
- **Data Fiduciary:** Any person who alone or in conjunction with other persons determines the purpose and means of processing of digital personal data.
- **Data Principal:** The individual to whom personal data relates.
- **Data Processor:** Any person who processes digital personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary.
- **Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF):** A Data Fiduciary notified as such by the Central Government based on volume, sensitivity, or potential risk of data processed.
- **Consent Manager:** A Data Fiduciary that enables a Data Principal to give, manage, review, and withdraw their consent through an interoperable platform.
- **Personal Data Breach:** Any unauthorized processing, accidental disclosure, acquisition, sharing, use, alteration, destruction, or loss of access to personal data.

---

## Chapter 2: Obligations of Data Fiduciaries

### Clause 2.1 – Notice to Data Principals
Every Data Fiduciary must provide a clear and plain language notice to Data Principals before or at the time of obtaining consent. The notice must specify:
- The nature of personal data to be collected
- The purpose for which data is to be processed
- The manner in which the Data Principal may exercise their rights
- The way in which consent may be withdrawn

### Clause 2.2 – Consent Requirements
Consent for processing personal data must be:
- Free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous
- Obtained through a clear affirmative action
- Verifiable and maintained in audit-ready format
- Separate for each distinct purpose of processing

### Clause 2.3 – Deemed Consent
Personal data may be processed without explicit consent in the following circumstances:
- For the State to provide subsidies, benefits, or services
- For compliance with any law or judgment of a court
- For responding to a medical emergency
- For purposes of employment subject to reasonable expectation

### Clause 2.4 – Data Accuracy and Storage Limitation
Data Fiduciaries must ensure accuracy of personal data and not retain it beyond the period necessary for the specified purpose. Upon completion of purpose, data must be deleted or anonymized within 3 months.

---

## Chapter 3: Rights of Data Principals

### Clause 3.1 – Right to Access
Every Data Principal has the right to obtain information about the personal data being processed, the purposes of processing, and the list of all third parties with whom personal data has been shared.

### Clause 3.2 – Right to Correction and Erasure
Data Principals have the right to correct inaccurate or misleading personal data and to have their personal data erased. The Data Fiduciary must act on such requests within 30 days.

### Clause 3.3 – Right to Grievance Redressal
Every Data Principal has the right to have their grievances redressed. Each Data Fiduciary must provide a mechanism to raise grievances and respond within 48 hours with resolution timelines.

### Clause 3.4 – Right to Nominate
Data Principals may nominate another individual to exercise rights on their behalf in the event of death or incapacity.

---

## Chapter 4: Obligations of Significant Data Fiduciaries

### Clause 4.1 – Data Protection Officer
Every Significant Data Fiduciary must appoint a Data Protection Officer (DPO) who shall:
- Be resident in India
- Be accountable to the Board of Directors
- Be the point of contact for Data Principals and Data Protection Board

### Clause 4.2 – Data Protection Impact Assessment
Significant Data Fiduciaries must conduct a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) before undertaking any processing activity likely to result in high risk to Data Principals. DPIA reports must be submitted to the Data Protection Board on request.

### Clause 4.3 – Algorithmic Transparency
Significant Data Fiduciaries processing data for automated decision-making that significantly affects Data Principals must provide an explanation of the logic involved and allow contest of decisions.

### Clause 4.4 – Periodic Audit
Significant Data Fiduciaries must conduct annual data protection audits through an independent auditor registered with the Data Protection Board.

---

## Chapter 5: Cross-Border Data Transfers

### Clause 5.1 – Transfer Restrictions
Personal data of Indian citizens may be transferred to countries or territories outside India only to those notified by the Central Government as having adequate data protection standards. The list of permitted countries will be maintained by MeitY.

### Clause 5.2 – Standard Contractual Clauses
Where cross-border transfers are permitted, they must be governed by Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) approved by the Data Protection Board, ensuring equivalent levels of protection.

### Clause 5.3 – Financial Data Localization
Personal financial data including bank account details, credit scores, transaction history, and loan data must be stored exclusively within India. This requirement supersedes cross-border transfer permissions for financial data categories.

---

## Chapter 6: Children's Data

### Clause 6.1 – Age Verification
Data Fiduciaries must implement reliable age verification mechanisms before processing personal data of children (under 18 years). Verifiable parental consent must be obtained before processing.

### Clause 6.2 – Prohibited Processing
No personal data of children shall be processed for:
- Behavioral monitoring or tracking
- Targeted advertising
- Profiling or psychographic assessment
- Any processing likely to cause detrimental effect on the child's wellbeing

### Clause 6.3 – Age-Appropriate Design
Digital products and services accessible to children must implement age-appropriate design standards including highest privacy settings by default.

---

## Chapter 7: Data Breach Management

### Clause 7.1 – Breach Notification to Data Protection Board
Every Data Fiduciary must notify the Data Protection Board of any personal data breach within 72 hours of becoming aware. The notification must include:
- Nature and extent of data affected
- Likely consequences of the breach
- Measures taken or proposed to mitigate the breach

### Clause 7.2 – Breach Notification to Data Principals
Where a personal data breach is likely to result in high risk to Data Principals, the Data Fiduciary must notify affected Data Principals without undue delay, in plain language, describing the nature of the breach and protective measures they can take.

### Clause 7.3 – Breach Register
Every Data Fiduciary must maintain a register of all personal data breaches whether or not notification was required, for a minimum period of 5 years.

---

## Chapter 8: Data Protection Board

### Clause 8.1 – Jurisdiction
The Data Protection Board of India shall adjudicate complaints relating to violations of the DPDP Act and these Rules. Complaints may be filed by Data Principals or the Central Government.

### Clause 8.2 – Penalties
- Failure to take reasonable security safeguards: Up to ₹250 crore
- Failure to notify the Board or Data Principals of a breach: Up to ₹200 crore
- Non-fulfilment of Data Principal rights: Up to ₹150 crore
- Breach of children's data provisions: Up to ₹200 crore
- Maximum penalty for any entity: ₹500 crore per instance

## BankPolicy: SBI Data Privacy Compliance Framework

### Policy PF-01: Consent Management Architecture
SBI must implement a centralized Consent Management System (CMS) capable of recording, managing, and auditing all data processing consents. The CMS must support granular consent capture at the level of individual data category and processing purpose.

### Policy PF-02: Data Classification Framework
All personal data processed by SBI must be classified as:
- Category A (Highly Sensitive): Biometric data, health data, financial transaction data
- Category B (Sensitive): Income data, employment history, credit scores
- Category C (General): Name, contact details, demographic information

### Policy PF-03: Data Retention Schedule
- Category A data: Retained for 8 years post account closure (as per RBI mandate), then deleted
- Category B data: Retained for 5 years post relationship end
- Category C data: Retained for 3 years post relationship end

### Policy PF-04: Third Party Data Processor Management
All third-party data processors including LSPs must sign Data Processing Agreements (DPAs) before receiving any personal data. DPAs must include security obligations, breach notification terms, and sub-processor restrictions.

### Policy PF-05: Data Localization Controls
All personal data of Indian residents must be processed and stored within India. Cloud services used for data storage must be certified for data residency in India. Cross-border transfers require DPO sign-off and Board-level approval.

## DataCategory: Classification Details

### DataCategory: BiometricData
Includes fingerprints, facial geometry, iris patterns, voice prints. Requires explicit written consent, highest encryption standard (AES-256), restricted access on need-to-know basis. Governed by UIDAI guidelines and DPDP Act Section 8.

### DataCategory: FinancialTransactionData
Includes bank statements, UPI transaction history, credit card spending patterns, EMI records. Subject to RBI data localization requirements. Retention: 8 years. Access restricted to credit risk and compliance teams.

### DataCategory: CreditScoreData
Includes CIBIL score, Experian score, internal bureau data. Access restricted during loan underwriting process. Cannot be shared with third parties without explicit consent. Subject to Right to Explanation under DPDP Rules.

### DataCategory: BehavioralData
Includes app usage patterns, click behavior, location history. Collection requires explicit consent per individual data type. Cannot be used for credit scoring without disclosure. Children's behavioral data collection is prohibited.

### DataCategory: DemographicData
Includes age, gender, address, PAN, Aadhaar number. Aadhaar data governed separately under Aadhaar Act 2016. PAN data subject to Income Tax Act restrictions. Retention as per Policy PF-03 Category C.

## Process: Compliance Workflows

### Process: ConsentCapture
Triggered when new customer onboards or new data processing purpose identified. Requires: KYC verification, purpose explanation, granular consent form, DPO review for sensitive data. Output: Signed consent record in CMS with timestamp and version.

### Process: DataBreachResponse
Triggered when any unauthorized data access detected. Step 1: Isolate affected systems. Step 2: Notify DPO within 1 hour. Step 3: Notify Data Protection Board within 72 hours. Step 4: Notify affected Data Principals if high risk. Step 5: Root cause analysis and remediation. Step 6: Update breach register.

### Process: DataSubjectRequestHandling
Triggered when Data Principal exercises right to access, correction, erasure, or portability. Response SLA: 48 hours acknowledgment, 30 days resolution. Escalation to DPO if complex. Legal review required for erasure requests conflicting with regulatory retention mandates.
